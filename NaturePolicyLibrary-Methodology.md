# **The Nature Policy Library \- Methodology** 

### 

### **About** 

The Nature Policy Library is a collection of national laws, policies and UN country submissions relevant to biodiversity and nature, hosted on the Climate Policy Radar app.

This page provides information about the scope and structure of the library, the definitions and classifications we use, our data collection methods and the limitations of our dataset. As we expand coverage and refine our approach, this methodology document will evolve to reflect developments.

For citing, please go to our [Terms of use](https://app.climatepolicyradar.org/terms-of-use). 

### **Scope of documents included**

Documents in the Nature Policy Library are tagged with the Theme "Nature".

The 23 targets of the [Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework](https://www.cbd.int/gbf/targets) (GBF) provide the strict bounds for inclusion. Because the GBF targets span a breadth of issues, such as conservation, pollution, health, and finance, using them as the basis for this scope means the library is cross-sectoral by design and directly relevant to national implementation of the framework.

For this collection, we have given particular focus to documents related to nature-based solutions initially. These are areas with clear overlaps with climate policy, building out from a core focus of Climate Policy Radar, that cut across topics and sectors including conservation and restoration, forestry, agriculture, wetlands, fisheries and blue carbon management. 

We include laws and policies that are either (a) demonstrably motivated by biodiversity or nature concerns, or (b) govern a priority sector that directly affects species or ecosystems, even where the document does not use explicit biodiversity language. For example, a national forestry law that regulates logging is included even if it does not refer to biodiversity.

We only include laws and policies that have full legal force, having passed through the legislature or an executive decision-making body, and/or that set out a current government policy objective or set of policy objectives.

### **Data collection methods**

Coverage currently builds on the laws and policies already held in the Climate Policy Radar database, alongside new documents sourced specifically for this collection by drawing on data from trusted data partners, such as [Nature4Climate](https://nature4climate.org). Our current coverage has also been reviewed against the scope criteria above and, where relevant, the nature label applied in order to surface these documents within our nature policy library.

### **Geographic coverage**

The library currently covers national-level documents from around the world. Subnational laws and policies are not included in this first phase. 

### **Overlap with climate law and policy**

Nature and climate law and policy often overlap. Many documents that govern ecosystems, such as forestry, agriculture, wetland or coastal management laws, are also relevant to climate mitigation and adaptation. As a result, some documents are relevant to both policy areas and may appear in both the Nature Policy Library and the wider Climate Policy Radar database.

**Principles and limitations**

The Nature Policy Library does not yet provide a comprehensive picture of nature and biodiversity-related law and policy globally. Whilst attempts have been made to capture policies and laws beyond strictly biodiversity-focused instruments, the library focuses predominantly on documents with nature-forward language in their titles or sourced from biodiversity-specific UN processes (CBD). This means relevant policies and pieces of legislation focused on key sectors that impact biodiversity (e.g. construction, or water) may currently be underrepresented. 

Some documents in the Nature Policy Library do not yet have a summary. We are working to add summaries to these documents as the library develops. Many documents in the library are drawn from the [Climate Change Laws of the World](https://climate-laws.org/) (CCLW) dataset, maintained by the Grantham Research Institute on Climate Change and the Environment at the London School of Economics. Summaries for these documents were written for the CCLW dataset and highlight the climate relevance of each document. As a result, they may not fully capture the document's relevance to biodiversity and nature. Please refer to the CCLW [methodology](https://climate-laws.org/methodology) for more information about how these summaries are written.

Coverage is currently limited to national-level policy. Document types skew towards public policy and public finance mechanisms \- laws, strategies, and plans \- with private sector obligations, voluntary commitments, and industry standards largely absent. 

Geographically, the library is slightly weighted towards Europe (particularly the EU), Latin America and sub-Saharan Africa, with thinner coverage from South Asia, the Middle East, and North America.

We will continue to broaden both coverage and scope and we welcome collaboration, contributions and feedback.

| Category | Definition |
| ----- | ----- |
| Law | Documents enacted by the legislative branch of a national government. |
| Policy | Documents approved by the executive branch of a national government. |
| UN submission (CBD) | Submissions from Parties to the UN Convention on Biological Diversity, such as National Biodiversity Strategies and Action Plans (NBSAPs), National Targets and National Reports. |
| UN submission (UNCCD) | Submissions from Parties to the UN Convention to Combat Desertification, such as voluntary Land Degradation Neutrality Target commitments and Drought Plans. |
