# **The Subnational Policy Library \- Methodology** 

* Scope of documents included  
  * The Subnational Policy Library focuses on the high-level, overarching climate response of states and regions, meaning documents like their climate change act, climate action plan or net zero strategy. Where a state or region also has a dedicated high-level mitigation or adaptation strategy, we include those alongside the overarching climate change framework. This means a state or region may have several associated documents. We do not include sector-specific policies in this initial launch of data.   
  * We only include laws and policies that are currently *active,* meaning documents must have full legal force, having passed through the legislature or through an executive decision-making body, and/or set out a current governmental policy objective or set of policy objectives.  
* Data collection process  
  * This data has been provided by organisations working at the forefront of subnational climate action, see listed below. Prior to their ingestion into the database, entries have gone through thorough data quality checks to ensure they meet our criteria for a high standard of data quality (e.g. source URL, DD/MM/YYYY). We also removed duplicates and standardised metadata such as document type (see definitions and classifications below).

* Sub/national scope  
  * We used the ISO 3166-2 which identifies the subdivisions of countries coded in ISO 3166-1 (though not all countries have ISO 3166-2 entries). It defines unique alphanumeric codes for the principal administrative subdivisions, such as states, provinces, regions, or counties, of all countries listed in ISO 3166-1. It uses a two-part format: country code \+ subdivision code (e.g. US-CA for California) and covers 5,000+ subdivisions across ISO 3166-1 geographies.  
* Definitions and classifications  
  * Category  
    * Law: we categorise documents as “Law” if they are enacted by the legislative branch of a subnational government.  
    * Policy: we categorise documents as “Policy” if they are approved by the executive branch of a subnational government.  
  * Document types  
    * State climate law: Climate laws are binding legislative instruments rather than executive strategies. They are distinguished by their legal form, having been enacted by a state legislature, and by their function: such laws typically include binding targets, establish institutions or oversight bodies, assign duties to public authorities, and mandate the preparation and periodic revision of action plans.  
    * State climate action plan: A climate action plan is a cross-cutting strategic instrument through which a subnational government sets out its climate response as a whole. Such plans generally establish economy-wide greenhouse gas emissions reduction targets, identify the sectoral measures required to meet them, and address resilience and adaptation alongside associated clean energy, economic and social objectives.   
    * State mitigation plan: A mitigation plan is a response directed specifically at the reduction or removal of greenhouse gas emissions. Documents of this type are characterised by emissions reduction targets, renewable energy investment, and commitments to phase out fossil fuel generation.  
    * State adaptation plan: An adaptation plan is a response directed at managing the impacts of climate change and strengthening resilience. Documents of this type are characterised by a climate risk and vulnerability assessment, sectoral resilience measures, and hazard-specific planning addressing climate risks like wildfire, extreme heat, drought, flooding, and slow onset changes like sea-level rise.  
* Principles and limitations  
  * This dataset includes subnational data from across 130+ subdivisions, focused on documents detailing their overarching climate response. The absence of data from a specific subdivision, does not mean there is no climate-relevant data for this specific subdivision.
